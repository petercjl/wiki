All the advantages of digital editing can give an 
intoxicating sense of freedom, particularly to editors 
who have been filing trims and rewinding reels for 
many years. But that sudden rush of freedom can be 
deceiving. As with every tool, the features of digital 
editing can cut both ways, and some of its most ad- 
mired qualities have hidden, dark sides that can hurt 
if you are not aware of them in advance. 
Let me explain what I mean with a few examples. 
Random Access and Speed 
One of the things I have not yet solved with digital 
editing, ironically, is how to deal most effectively with 
its central advantage-instant random-access. 
Computerized editing systems achieve most of their 
speed by retrieving the requested material instanta- 
neously, which is what we mean by "instant random- 
access." This allows the editor to do such things as 
effortlessly compare line readings from different takes. 
But random-access ultimately depends on knowing 
exactly what you want.. . and that is not always the 
case, as any editor can tell you.
